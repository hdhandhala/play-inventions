Immersive VR Powertrain Lab

A prototype VR lab where learners carry out real assembly processes of electric drives, with input from Bosch, VDC Fellbach and XR-UpSkills.

Unity (C#) · XR Interaction Toolkit · OpenXR · URP · Meta Quest · Blender · Cinema 4D

Four stations, learned by doing

Each station is a real process step. Learners do the work themselves; the lab checks every step and explains what went wrong.

Wire winding

Fix the wire, wind it with controlled rotation, cut it and place the coil. The wound wire is a live-generated helix that follows the reel, and a cut splits off a tail that falls away physically.

Procedural meshReel inertiaHaptics

Drilling & screwing

The hole deepens with real drill penetration, not a timer. A screw seats only when tip and shaft are aligned, then advances only as the screwdriver is twisted, with a ratchet-style direction lock.

Penetration-basedTwist-drivenDepth haptics

Positioning & inspection

Components must be placed within position and rotation tolerances. Feedback tells the learner whether the position, the orientation or the order of steps is wrong.

TolerancesDiagnostic feedback

Battery gluing

Apply glue with the glue gun, place each battery on the highlighted pad slot and wait for it to cure. A timed step teaches that the process needs patience, not just the right order.

Guided slotsTimed curing

Physics first, not snap joints

Typical VR app

An object gets close to its target and snaps into place. Quick to build, but the learner never feels the actual process.

My lab

Progress comes from the learner's own movement: turning the screwdriver drives the screw, rotating the reel winds the wire, a handwheel turns only around its axis.

Every step follows the same loop

Watch (optional)→Try→Check→Feedback→Next step

Not right yet? A specific hint, and the learner tries again.

Haptics everywhere

Vibration follows what happens – drill depth, reel speed, the cut – and goes to the hand holding the tool.

Standalone performance

Colliders, rigidbodies and constraints tuned for stable interaction at a steady frame rate on Meta Quest.

Clean C# architecture

Reusable components and clear interaction states, from idle to completed. The core scripts are on GitHub.

Guidance

A holographic instructor, there when you need one

A life-size hologram demonstrates each step right in the workspace. It's off by default: learners explore first and call it up when they're stuck.

  • Play, pause, step back and forward, then "Mark as Done"
  • A step panel lists the procedure and tracks progress
  • Instruction sequences produced in Cinema 4D and anchored at each station
Research project · FAU Erlangen-Nürnberg · 2024–2025

VR learning platform – does it work?

A walk-through VR classroom on EV fundamentals in four zones, compared with traditional material in a pilot study with 5 learners per group.

Onboarding
Theory
Hands-on
Assessment

Knowledge test

same 15 questions after ~20 min of learning

VR group averaged about 8.5 / 15, and none of the 5 VR learners scored below 7.5. In the document group, 2 of 5 did.

What VR helped with

question-level patterns

VR learners did better on spatial and component questions: where parts sit and how they work together. Document readers did slightly better on text-based theory.

What learners asked for

questionnaire feedback

More control over the pace of narration, which shaped the on-demand guidance in my Master's thesis.

Pilot study (n = 10): indicative results, not proof. Long-term retention is still open.
